About me

I came to data science through mathematics—and stayed for the questions data can answer.

My path has taken me from studying mathematics in Rwanda to working with real operational data, and now to studying uncertainty and statistical learning in Nice.

My background

I started with Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Rwanda, where I learned to enjoy the reasoning behind a model as much as its results. At AIMS Rwanda, I moved deeper into data science and graduated with honours. My AIMS thesis challenged me to compare deep generative models with classical forecasting on Bitcoin price data—and to be honest about where simpler methods performed better. That experience shaped how I think about AI: a model has to earn our trust through evidence. I am now in the second year of an Erasmus Mundus Master's in Mathematical Modelling at Université Côte d'Azur in Nice, after completing my first year at the University of L'Aquila in Italy.

What motivates me is the part of data science that happens before a polished result appears: asking whether the data is reliable, choosing the right method, and making findings useful to someone. At Cenfri Rwanda, I wrote SQL queries and built checks and dashboards around six dimensions of data quality—accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness and validity. At One Acre Fund, I cleaned agricultural survey data and helped turn seasonal indicators into dashboards for monitoring teams. Earlier work with customer accounts at Sanlam Vie and technology-sector analysis at Rwanda ICT Chamber gave me a practical appreciation for detail, context and the people behind a dataset. I want to keep connecting that grounded experience with my research in stochastic modelling and machine learning.

Research direction

My main research question is how much confidence we should place in a prediction when the underlying system is complex and changing. I explored this in my AIMS thesis on nonlinear Bitcoin prices, and I am now studying statistical learning and stochastic methods more deeply. I am also interested in transport data and computer vision; my YOLOv8 traffic-detection work is one concrete step in that direction. These interests are evolving, and I distinguish completed projects from questions I hope to investigate next.
